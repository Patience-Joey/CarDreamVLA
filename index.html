v13 TEMPORAL SAFETY

Keep the policy frozen.
Bound the safety correction.

A separate temporal network combines four raster-LiDAR frames, semantic BEV, the frozen Dreamer latent, instruction ID, and base action.

Encode temporal risk

A CNN and GRU encode LiDAR history; fused features predict risk and a two-dimensional safe action.

Confirm before intervening

A separately trained LiDAR anchor uses validation-calibrated thresholds, smoothing, and consecutive-frame confirmation.

Apply bounded correction

Confirmed risk activates bounded longitudinal and steering corrections. Outside active intervals, the frozen base action is preserved.

LONGTAILBENCH

Rare events.
Explicit evaluation.

Twenty parameterized scenario types across four CARLA towns, organized with ISO 34504 scenario categories.

Every predeclared eligible episode counts.

Scenario-specific checks verify whether the intended event occurred. Binary safety outcomes use all topology-feasible predeclared launches, including untriggered or unaccepted scenarios. Strict acceptance is reported as exposure, rather than used to filter safety outcomes.

THE PAPER

Abstract

Route-following accuracy does not measure how an autonomous driving policy responds to rare hazards. We introduce LongTailBench, a CARLA evaluation with 20 scenario types across four towns, and use it to separate route execution, hazard recognition, and closed-loop reaction. In a strict intention-to-treat comparison of 450 topology-feasible episodes per policy, adding route-maneuver tokens to a DreamerV3 policy does not improve long-tail safety. An instruction-ID-conditioned student distilled from a 7B vision-language teacher exposes a partly decodable hazard signal, but its original camera-based reaction head and maneuver-token override do not establish a closed-loop gain. We therefore evaluate a separate CarDreamVLA v13 Temporal Safety module that combines four-frame raster LiDAR, bird's-eye view, a frozen Dreamer latent, instruction ID, and the base action. A calibrated LiDAR risk anchor activates bounded action correction while the base policy remains frozen. Across four held-out evaluation seeds and 240 episodes per method, hazard-attributed collisions fall from 17.5% to 10.0%, and near misses from 30.8% to 23.3%; internal path progress changes from 0.106 to 0.101. The result supports a safety improvement within the evaluated CARLA policy and sensor configuration. Comparisons with released LMDrive and TransFuser systems use a common LongTailBench protocol and retain their native sensors and controllers.
